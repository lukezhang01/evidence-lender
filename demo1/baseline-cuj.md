# Baseline critical user journey

How our persona tries to get a small loan today, without our product. This is the baseline we measure against.

## Persona

**Diego Alvarez, 31.** Moved to Toronto from Bogotá eight months ago on a work permit. Works as a data analyst and is paid every two weeks into a Canadian chequing account. Has about $40,000 in savings and owns an apartment back in Colombia. Has paid rent on time every month since arriving.

He has no Canadian credit file. To a Canadian lender he looks like someone with no history at all, even though his account shows steady pay, regular rent and money left over every month. His savings and property abroad don't count, because a Canadian lender can't see or check them.

## Goal

Borrow $4,000 for a used car so he can take a better-paid job outside transit range. He wants a fair answer within a week, based on the money he actually earns and keeps here, without wiring his savings out of Colombia.

## Journey audit

Times are our estimates. A context switch is a change of tool or channel.

| # | Action | Where | Context switch | Time | Friction | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Searches for loans for people with no credit history and compares lenders | Browser | — | 45 min | Medium | Almost every option starts with a credit check |
| 2 | Applies online at his own bank for a $4,000 personal loan | Bank website | Browser → bank site | 20 min | Low | Standard form; asks for his SIN for the credit check |
| 3 | Gets an automatic decline: "insufficient credit history" | Email | Bank site → email | Instant | Critical | The decision never looked at his account, at the same bank |
| 4 | Calls the bank to ask why. The agent can't override it and books a branch appointment | Phone | Email → phone | 40 min, then 6 days until the appointment | High | Nobody can explain what would change the answer |
| 5 | Asks HR for an employment letter | Work email | Phone → work email | 10 min, then 3 days | Medium | Waiting on someone else |
| 6 | Downloads pay stubs, and his Colombian bank statements, which are in Spanish | Payroll portal, Colombian bank's site | 2 switches | 45 min | Medium | Foreign statements need translation, and may not be accepted at all |
| 7 | Brings passport, work permit, employment letter, pay stubs and statements to the branch | Branch | Online → in person | 1.5 h with travel | High | The advisor says foreign assets can't be used, and underwriting still needs a bureau file |
| 8 | Waits for a second decision | Email | — | 5 days | High | No status or timeline |
| 9 | Is offered a secured credit card ($500 limit, $500 deposit) instead of the loan | Email | — | — | Critical | Doesn't meet the goal; building a file this way takes months |
| 10 | Looks at alternative lenders, or asks family to wire money | Browser, WhatsApp, wire transfer | 2 switches | 1 h, then 3 days for a wire | High | High-cost credit, or using the savings he wanted to keep |

## Totals

| Measure | Baseline |
|---|---|
| Steps | 10 |
| Context switches | 9 |
| Active time | About 5 hours |
| Elapsed time | About 3 weeks |
| Critical friction points | 2 (steps 3 and 9) |
| Outcome | No loan at a fair price |

## Where it breaks

Both critical points have the same cause: the decision rests on a credit file Diego doesn't have, and never reads the account that shows he can repay. Everything after step 3 is him trying to prove something his bank statement already shows.
